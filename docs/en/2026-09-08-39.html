Unwanted CPR Still Happening: What's Blocking Patient Autonomy in Japanese Hospitals?

スポンサーリンク

An investigation reveals that cardiopulmonary resuscitation is being performed on patients who did not desire it, raising questions about informed consent and end-of-life care practices in Japanese medical institutions. The findings highlight inconsistent protocols for obtaining patient wishes, the gap between family expectations and patient preferences, and insufficient ethical support systems for medical staff. The lack of standardized advance directive procedures across facilities has become a critical issue.

Key Points

  • Wide variations exist among medical institutions in confirming patient preferences, indicating the need for unified guidelines on advance directives and informed consent procedures
  • A significant disconnect often exists between family decision-making and patients' actual wishes, particularly in end-of-life scenarios, revealing the need for improved patient-family-physician communication
  • Insufficient training and ethical support systems in many healthcare facilities may contribute to default full resuscitation protocols that override documented patient preferences
