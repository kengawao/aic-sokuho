Following severe flooding in Himi City, Toyama Prefecture last month, medical teams conducted screenings for deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism risks among shelter residents. The prolonged sedentary lifestyle and limited mobility in evacuation centers create dangerous conditions for blood clot formation. Health authorities are now prioritizing preventive measures and early detection in disaster response protocols.

Key Points

  • Extended shelter stays create high-risk environments for DVT due to immobility and dehydration, conditions that promote thrombosis formation
  • Early screening by medical professionals is critical for preventing life-threatening pulmonary embolism complications in displaced populations
  • Future disaster shelter management must emphasize physical activity and health monitoring as essential components of emergency response infrastructure
