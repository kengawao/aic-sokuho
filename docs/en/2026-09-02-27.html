Moyamoya disease, characterized by abnormal narrowing of cerebral blood vessels, leaves many patients grappling with lasting complications even after surgery. Patients employ creative lifestyle adaptations while managing symptoms like paralysis and speech disorders. The case highlights that medical treatment alone is insufficient—comprehensive rehabilitation and psychological support are equally critical for improving patients' quality of life.

Key Points

  • Post-surgical complications of moyamoya disease persist and significantly impact patients' daily lives
  • Patient-driven lifestyle adaptations and coping strategies are as vital as medical interventions
  • Society needs stronger support systems to assist patients living with chronic after-effects
