スポンサーリンク

Japan's public pension system reported record accumulated reserves of over 302 trillion yen for fiscal year 2025, driven by strong investment returns and rising wages. While the numbers appear encouraging, experts warn that demographic challenges from declining birth rates and an aging population threaten the system's long-term sustainability. The surplus masks underlying structural problems that may require significant reforms.

Key Points

  • Strong investment performance in equity markets was the primary driver of fund growth, raising questions about sustainability during market downturns
  • Wage increases boosted insurance premium revenues, but the shrinking working-age population creates an unsustainable support ratio between contributors and beneficiaries
  • Large accumulated reserves alone cannot ensure future solvency without addressing Japan's demographic crisis and potential policy reforms
