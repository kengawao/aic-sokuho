From Apartment to Hotel? The Hidden Legal Gray Zone Behind Japan's Booming Minpaku Problem

国内 2026-09-05 • Source: マンション一室で旅館業 民泊問題

スポンサーリンク

Unauthorized minpaku (short-term rental) operations in single apartment units are becoming increasingly problematic in Japan. While the Hotel Business Law requires proper licensing, enforcement varies by municipality and regulations struggle to keep pace. Resident conflicts over noise, garbage, and security are escalating, yet many building owners rely on minpaku income, creating tension between legal compliance and economic necessity.

Key Points

  • The Hotel Business Law mandates licensing, but most apartments lack the required fire safety equipment and minimum area specifications, making compliance technically infeasible
  • The boundary between the Residential Lodging Business Law (allowing up to 180 days annually with notification) and the Hotel Business Law remains ambiguous, creating loopholes
  • Rising resident complaints—noise, security risks, waste management—must be balanced against property owners' need for revenue in an aging, vacancy-plagued housing market
