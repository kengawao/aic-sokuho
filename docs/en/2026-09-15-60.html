スポンサーリンク

Replacement bus services introduced following railway line closures in rural Japan are facing severe financial difficulties, prompting municipal governments to consider shutting them down. Significant discrepancies between projected and actual ridership have caused rapid deficits, with dwindling populations exacerbating the problem. Experts point to inadequate feasibility studies and unrealistic route planning at the time of rail closures, raising fundamental questions about balancing regional mobility needs with economic viability.

Key Points

  • Replacement bus services are failing to meet projected passenger numbers, leading to budget deficits within years of their launch
  • Structural decline in demand due to population loss makes financial sustainability increasingly difficult, regardless of operational efficiency
  • The initial decision-making process for rail closures appears to have lacked thorough long-term analysis of replacement transport viability
