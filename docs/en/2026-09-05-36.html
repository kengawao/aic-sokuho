Japan's National Police Agency plans to strengthen driving proficiency tests, particularly targeting elderly drivers with suspected cognitive decline. The move aims to improve road safety but raises concerns about implementation costs, facility shortages, and impacts on rural residents who lack alternative transportation. Policy details are still under review.

Key Points

  • Enhanced cognitive and driving proficiency tests target elderly drivers amid rising accident concerns, though accident statistics reveal complex patterns
  • Stricter testing requirements could reduce road safety incidents but may disproportionately affect rural communities with limited public transport alternatives
  • Implementation challenges include insufficient test facilities, examiner training, and the need for clear, objective assessment criteria
