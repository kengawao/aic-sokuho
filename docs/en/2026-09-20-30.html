Japan's Population Shift: 1 in 3 Now Elderly, With 9.43 Million Seniors Still in the Workforce

国内 2026-09-20 • Source: 高齢者の割合 総人口の29.6％で過去最高 総務省

スポンサーリンク

According to Statistics Bureau data released on Respect for the Aged Day, Japan's senior population aged 65 and over has reached a record 29.6% of the total population. Simultaneously, the number of employed seniors hit an all-time high of 9.43 million workers. This demographic transformation reflects both the nation's aging society and a growing reliance on elderly labor as the younger workforce shrinks. The trend raises critical questions about the sustainability of social security systems and intergenerational labor market dynamics.

Key Points

  • Elderly population now comprises 29.6% of Japan's total—nearly one in three citizens—marking a historic demographic milestone
  • Employment among seniors reached 9.43 million, the highest on record, driven by extended retirement ages and rehiring policies
  • The sustainability of relying on elderly workers to offset labor shortages becomes increasingly uncertain as the workforce ages
