Touchscreen ordering systems are spreading rapidly across Japanese restaurants, but customer feedback reveals significant dissatisfaction. While establishments promote convenience, users complain about operational hassles, interface difficulties, and reduced interaction with staff. The gap between corporate cost-cutting goals and actual customer experience has become increasingly apparent, sparking industry-wide debate.

Key Points

  • Touchscreen ordering prioritizes labor cost reduction and operational efficiency over customer satisfaction improvements
  • Elderly and less tech-savvy users face high barriers to entry, making UI/UX design a critical factor for inclusive service
  • Tension between reducing human interaction costs and maintaining service quality—a fundamental challenge for modern hospitality
