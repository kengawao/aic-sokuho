Japan's food service sector is experiencing a divergence between high-end dining and budget-friendly options. As the market recovers from COVID-19, consumer preferences have split into distinct categories. While premium restaurants are thriving, low-cost establishments are also expanding—but mid-range dining faces increasing pressure. Labor shortages and rising ingredient costs are forcing businesses to choose between premium positioning and cost-leadership strategies.

Key Points

  • Consumer demand polarization accelerates growth in luxury and budget segments while squeezing mid-market restaurants
  • Mid-range dining establishments face existential pressure to either upgrade or downgrade their positioning
  • Labor scarcity and commodity inflation force restaurants to adopt specialized, rather than generalist, business models
