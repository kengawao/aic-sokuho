On September 1st, Japan's National Foundation Day for Disaster Prevention, the government conducted a comprehensive disaster response drill simulating a major earthquake along the Japan and Kuril Trenches. The scenario involved catastrophic damage across Hokkaido and the Tohoku region. Government agencies and local authorities practiced initial response procedures, coordination protocols, and information sharing mechanisms to ensure effective disaster management and identify areas for improvement.

Key Points

  • The Japan and Kuril Trenches have produced massive earthquakes in the past, with high probability of major seismic activity within the next 30 years
  • A large-scale disaster scenario affecting Hokkaido and Tohoku underscores the urgent need to strengthen disaster preparedness systems in these regions
  • Effective coordination between national and local governments and streamlined information protocols are critical factors that determine disaster response efficiency
