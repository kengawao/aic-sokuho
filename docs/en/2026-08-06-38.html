スポンサーリンク

Artificial intelligence-powered motion analysis is increasingly being adopted in Japanese high school baseball. By analyzing pitching mechanics and running techniques, coaches can now provide data-driven, individualized training that has produced measurable results—some pitchers have gained over 10 km/h in fastball velocity. However, the high cost of these systems is creating a growing gap between well-funded schools and those with limited resources. Questions also remain about long-term effectiveness and injury prevention compared to traditional coaching methods.

Key Points

  • AI motion analysis enables objective, data-driven coaching tailored to individual athletes, moving away from traditional trial-and-error methods
  • High implementation costs risk widening the gap between wealthy schools with access to AI tools and underfunded schools, raising equity concerns across the sport
  • Long-term effectiveness and injury prevention outcomes remain understudied; short-term velocity gains may not translate to sustained career success or athlete safety
