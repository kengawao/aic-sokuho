The European Union has decided to invest approximately 3.6 billion yen in Greenland, marking a strategic move to secure resources and strengthen logistics in the Arctic region. This investment is driven by climate change improving Arctic accessibility, and geopolitical competition with the US, Russia, and China over Arctic dominance and resources.

Key Points

  • Climate change is making Arctic resources more accessible, increasing the strategic and economic value of Arctic infrastructure and shipping routes
  • The US, Russia, and China are also intensifying their Arctic engagement, making it crucial for the EU to secure its own influence in the region
  • While Greenland is part of Denmark, it has significant autonomy, raising questions about how EU investment strategies will function in practice
