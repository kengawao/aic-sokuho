Why Japanese Companies Are Now Competing for National Institute of Technology Graduates

経済 2026-10-04 • Source: 企業は「高専卒」に熱視線か 背景

スポンサーリンク

Japanese corporations are increasingly seeking graduates from Kosen (National Institute of Technology), five-year technical schools that emphasize practical skills. As university enrollment rates rise while Kosen applicants decline, employers face a paradox: growing demand for immediately productive technical talent but shrinking supply. This shift reflects changing workforce needs, with manufacturers and IT companies offering improved compensation packages to attract Kosen graduates over four-year university graduates.

Key Points

  • Immediate job-readiness: Five years of hands-on technical education produces graduates with specialized expertise, often surpassing four-year university graduates in practical capabilities
  • Market inversion: Declining Kosen applicant numbers contradict rising corporate hiring demand, creating a seller's market for graduates
  • Compensation improvements: Shift from seniority-based wages toward merit-based systems increases the relative value of highly skilled technical professionals
