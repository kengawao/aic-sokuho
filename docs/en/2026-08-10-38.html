The Ghost Towns of Greed: Why Bubble-Era Resort Condos Are Still Abandoned Decades Later

経済 2026-08-10 • Source: 廃虚も バブルで乱立したリゾマン

スポンサーリンク

Resort condominiums ("resomans") constructed en masse during Japan's 1980s bubble economy are increasingly sitting vacant and deteriorating. After the bubble's collapse, the collapse in demand left thousands of these properties unsellable, trapping owners in a financial nightmare. Owners face mounting maintenance fees, inheritance complications, and effectively worthless assets—creating a compound crisis that has left entire resort communities in decay, decades after construction.

Key Points

  • The structural legacy of 1980s speculative real estate excess continues to function as long-term economic burden, revealing the dangers of bubble-driven overbuilding
  • Property owners face a legal and financial bind: unpaid management fees, unclear inheritance status, and homes worth far less than maintenance costs create unsolvable situations
  • Local governments and private initiatives have limited tools to address the crisis; systemic policy reform and new disposal mechanisms are urgently needed
