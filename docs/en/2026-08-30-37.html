Alpine Slopes Turn Brown: Why Has Sika Deer Overpopulation in the Southern Japanese Alps Become So Severe?

国内 2026-08-30 • Source: 山肌埋め尽くすシカ 南アルプス

スポンサーリンク

The Southern Japanese Alps are experiencing severe vegetation damage from an exploding sika deer population, with hillsides becoming completely denuded of plants and turning brown. The dramatic overeating by deer has led to soil exposure and prevented vegetation recovery. This ecological imbalance raises concerns about erosion and the cascade effects on wildlife. Managing deer numbers through more aggressive culling has become urgent, yet the aging of hunters and declining licensing rates pose significant obstacles.

Key Points

  • Exponential deer population growth: Without natural predators and due to warmer winters, sika deer numbers have surged beyond the capacity of current culling efforts to maintain balance
  • Cascading ecosystem collapse: Overgrazing has eliminated subalpine vegetation, affecting food sources for other wildlife and causing trail collapse and soil erosion in mountain areas
  • Implementation challenges: Effective regional deer management and expanded culling programs are necessary, but are hindered by an aging hunter population and fewer people obtaining hunting licenses
